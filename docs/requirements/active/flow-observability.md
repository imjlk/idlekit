# Flow observability

## The flow adapter is optional {#req-pr15-flow-adapter}

Requirement `REQ-PR15-FLOW-ADAPTER`. `PR-15` owns it.

`flowOptionalAdapter` is `observer-not-required-model`. Flow samples are optional. `RunObserver.onFlow` records one sample and does nothing when the callback is absent. A model gains no required method. Resource names stay in the fixture. The same adapter covers logistics, crafting, and expedition-slot samples. The report contract is `idlekit.flow.v1`. The observation contract is `idlekit.flow.observation.v1`. `externalProvider` is false. The structural schema is `typia.json.schema<FlowReportV1, "3.1">()`. `validateFlowReport` is `typia.createValidate<FlowReportV1>()`. Seed `15` labels `examples/analysis/flow-pipeline.json`. Amounts, edge ratios, and Little's law inputs use the decimal wire spelling, with an optional sign and exponent, such as `1e2` or `+2.0E1`. `parseFlowObservation` rejects a value that is not a finite non-negative decimal, a sample whose `endSec` is before its `startSec` or whose window length overflows a finite Number, a second sample with the same process, resource, kind, and window, a second edge with the same source and destination, a zero denominator, and a loss above 1, instead of letting the diagnosis drop it, read it by order, or derive a negative duration.

Flow diagnostics convert decimal wire values to JavaScript Number values. A mathematically nonzero decimal that converts to zero, such as `1e-400` or `2e-324`, is rejected. True zero spellings such as `0e-400` and `-0e-400` remain valid, and a positive representable subnormal such as `5e-324` is accepted. A loss that rounds to 1 is accepted only when its wire decimal is exactly 1; a smaller decimal that rounds to 1 is rejected.

The executed test is `declaresFlowAdapter` in `packages/core/src/sim/analysis/flow.test.ts`.

## A steady pipeline binds at the middle stage {#req-pr15-pipeline}

Requirement `REQ-PR15-PIPELINE`. `PR-15` owns it.

`flowPipelineConstraint` is `process-throughput-20`. A steady chain whose inflow is 100 per minute, whose middle stage throughput and capacity are 20 per minute, and whose sink capacity is 30 per minute with throughput 20 per minute, reports a process constraint. Final throughput stays 20 per minute. Doubling the inflow changes the queue and leaves final throughput at 20 per minute. A confirmed capacity constraint requires a causal model or experiment that names that saturated process. Without that causal record the same saturation is suspected. A confirmed hypothesis cites the causal record as `causal` in its evidence refs. Saturation time uses the evidence window. Capacity, upstream throughput, and downstream capacity are read for the resource each edge names and inside the throughput sample's window. A sample of another resource or another window does not bind. An observed downstream capacity must exceed everything that reaches it in that window, this process's forwarded amount plus every other feed, and an unobserved other feed leaves the slack unproven. That capacity must be in the destination's rate unit, its own throughput unit or this process's unit when the edge keeps the resource. An edge that keeps the resource keeps its rate unit, so an upstream source or a side feed in another unit is not counted as an offer or a load. The bind cites the edges, the downstream capacity, the throughput that named its unit, and the feeds it used. Every saturated process is a candidate. A process and resource saturated in several windows is judged on its latest saturated window before its evidence is read, so an older proven window does not stand in for a newer unproven one. The one a causal model or experiment names is confirmed and listed first, and the other binds stay suspected. Each terminal process reports a final throughput for every resource its incoming edges deliver, from its latest window. An upstream source may report its offer as throughput or, failing that, as inflow. A graph with edges but no terminal process, such as a cycle, reports no final throughput. A graph-derived final throughput cites the edges that deliver its resource. Without edges, every throughput series reports its latest window. Fact ids escape process and resource ids.

The executed test is `diagnosesSteadyPipeline` in `packages/core/src/sim/analysis/flow.test.ts`.

## Converted units stay apart {#req-pr15-conservation}

Requirement `REQ-PR15-CONSERVATION`. `PR-15` owns it.

`flowConservation` is `converted-units-stay-apart`. An edge with numerator 1, denominator 2, and loss 0.1 turns an inflow of 100 per minute into 45 per minute of the destination unit. Queue 40 plus unretained 5 matches that theoretical amount. Conservation compares rates: queue and unretained are read as per-window amounts in the destination rate unit, the unit of the conversion and of an observed destination throughput. A queue reported as a stock in item units, as the pipeline fixtures report their queue facts, is a fact but not a conservation input. Every fact names its process and its resource. The source unit is not added to the destination unit. A same-unit stock is not reported as a converted product. When the destination's throughput names its unit in the window, queue and unretained must be in that unit, and the fact cites that throughput. The source sample is read for the edge's source resource, and queue and unretained are read for its destination resource inside the source sample's window. Every source window is tried, throughput before inflow. Edges that deliver the same destination process and resource are summed against that one stock. When several windows conserve, the latest one is the fact. A conversion whose result overflows a finite number does not match; an intermediate product that leaves the finite range, such as `1e200` times `1e200` over `1e200`, does not make an in-range result unavailable. When a conversion of individually representable positive operands underflows to Number zero, it is unavailable and cannot establish conservation, even against an empty stock. A true zero source, zero numerator, or exactly total loss remains a valid zero conversion, even when the pre-loss product would overflow. Amounts are compared relatively, and zero matches only zero, so a positive amount of any size does not equal an empty stock.

The executed test is `checksFlowConservation` in `packages/core/src/sim/analysis/flow.test.ts`.

## A model without flow observations is unsupported {#req-pr15-unsupported}

Requirement `REQ-PR15-UNSUPPORTED`. `PR-15` owns it.

`flowUnsupportedModel` is `no-invented-stall`. A model that does not observe flow is status `unsupported` and has no numeric facts. A growth regime, including stall, is not a flow sample and is not mapped to a capacity bottleneck. `stallMapped` stays false.

The executed test is `leavesUnknownModelsUnsupported` in `packages/core/src/sim/analysis/flow.test.ts`.

## Facts stay apart from the cause {#req-pr15-suspected}

Requirement `REQ-PR15-SUSPECTED`. `PR-15` owns it.

`flowSuspectedCause` is `facts-apart-from-cause`. When a throughput sample has no capacity, starved time, or blocked time, demand shortage and equipment shortage stay unresolved: status `insufficient-data` and claim `none`. Starved time without a capacity bind is a suspected material shortage. Blocked time without a capacity bind is a suspected demand shortage. Starved and blocked time are judged per process: each process gets its own hypothesis, a bind on one process does not hide another process's wait, and a process with both stays unresolved with its own `insufficient-data` hypothesis. Little's law inputs whose product overflows a finite number are `insufficient-data`. Individually representable positive arrival and wait inputs whose product underflows to Number zero are also `insufficient-data`, even when inventory is zero. A truly zero arrival or wait retains a valid zero product. A wait is a duration in seconds, and a wait sample in another unit is rejected while parsing and is not evidence. The latest window of each wait kind is the evidence, every resource in that window counts, and a latest window with no positive wait clears an older one; that process then keeps an `insufficient-data` hypothesis citing the clearing samples. Little's law applies only on a steady window with a defined work flow, and a ramp-up marker on the observation keeps it off as well. It is not applied to ramp-up. A consistent Little's law result does not by itself confirm a cause. Facts and hypotheses are separate objects and link through evidence refs.

The executed test is `separatesFactsFromCauses` in `packages/core/src/sim/analysis/flow.test.ts`.
